The three workstreams

Workstream 1

A Paris entity that runs

Several new people will join a small office at the same time. Without simple basics, every hire and every invoice takes leadership time. Covers: People Operations and Financial Operations.

What I will look at first
  • How hiring, contracts and payroll work today, and who does them (Paris, London, external provider).
  • How budget, cash and vendor payments are tracked.
  • Which daily tasks reach the leadership team and should not.
Actions
  • One onboarding path for every new hire: contract, payroll, tool access, first-week plan.
  • A monthly rhythm: budget against actual, cash position, vendor payment calendar.
  • Short written policies: expenses, purchasing, approvals, leave.
  • Automation of repeated steps with no-code tools, where it really saves time.
What you get

A Paris operating manual (version 1) and a monthly operations report.

Main indicator

Days between a signed offer and a new hire who is fully operational.

Done before: At Voila Vinos, the company I co-founded, I managed cash, vendor payments, budget and HR myself, with more than 30 employees. At Golden Bees (Groupe Figaro), I saved 3 full-time positions at the same volume by automating operational workflows.

To check with you: what is already handled from London or by external providers.
Workstream 2

Fast customer onboarding, with full controls

Between a signed contract and a first payment there are checks, documents and handovers. This is where speed and compliance meet. Covers: Customer Operations.

What I will look at first
  • Each step from signed contract to first live payment, and the time each step takes.
  • Where files wait: missing documents, back-and-forth, handovers between Sales, Compliance and Operations.
  • The most frequent reasons for escalations.
Actions
  • A map of the journey with real times, shared with Compliance and Customer Success.
  • A clear document checklist for each client type, so clients send the right file the first time.
  • One owner and one visible status for each onboarding.
  • An escalation path with response times, and a monthly review of repeated issues.
What you get

An onboarding playbook and a tracking dashboard.

Main indicator

Days between a signed contract and the first live payment.

Done before: At Groupe Figaro, I restructured the Care team, rebuilt how accounts are assigned and deployed AI agents to automate support. At Golden Bees, churn went from 60% to 49% in 12 months through better service quality and client data dashboards.

To check with you: the current onboarding time and volumes, and which steps Compliance must fully own.
Workstream 3

A regulatory calendar where nothing slips

A regulated business has many deadlines: filings, reports, audits. The risk is not the rule itself, it is a date without a clear owner. Covers: Regulatory Operations.

What I will look at first
  • The full list of filings, reports and audits, with dates and current owners.
  • How deadlines are tracked today.
  • Who does what between the Regulatory Operations Lead, Compliance, Legal, Finance and this role.
Actions
  • One shared calendar, with an owner and a backup for every deadline.
  • A responsibility matrix agreed with the MLRO and the Regulatory Operations Lead.
  • Checklists and reminders before each deadline.
  • An audit-ready folder: evidence is stored when the work is done, not just before the audit.
What you get

A regulatory calendar and a responsibility matrix.

Main indicator

Share of regulatory deadlines that are ready at least one week early.

My honest position: I have not worked in a regulated fintech. The regulatory expertise stays with your Compliance team. What I bring is operating discipline: clear owners, KPI frameworks and processes that people really follow.

To check with you: how this role and the Regulatory Operations Lead share the work.
